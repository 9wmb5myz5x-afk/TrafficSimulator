| D5 | 2026-10-05 | The engine stays **Foundation-free with pure-Swift transcendental maths** (`DMath`). | libm differs between glibc and Darwin, so a Foundation-free engine is the only way to make a trace hash identical on Linux CI, macOS CI and devices. | D6 | 2026-10-05 | **Clock runs 8× faster than vehicles** (`clockScale = 8`: a day lasts 3 sim-hours); only **1/clockScale of residents drive on a given day** (`dailyDriverShare`, scaled by the demand slider). External through-traffic rates are per *simulated* hour. | Vehicles must move in real physical time, and a day must be playable. If everyone drove, road flows would be 8× those of a real town of the same size and every map jams. With the sample, a 2,500-resident suburb produces ≈ 850 veh/h at the AM peak, matching ITE trip-generation rates. |
| D7 | 2026-10-05 | **Regional commuting**: the share of workers employed beyond the map follows local job supply. They leave via regional connections and return later. | A bedroom suburb with ~200 local jobs cannot employ 1,500 workers; without this, all trips converged on a few shops. |
| D8 | 2026-10-05 | **Driveways are right-in/right-out** (kerb-side carriageway). A destination that can't be reached inside the map (e.g. a stub road that runs off the map edge) is reached by **turning round beyond the map edge** (leave, re-enter ~90 s later). | Avoids unrealistic left turns across traffic from driveways, and keeps every building reachable. |
| D9 | 2026-10-05 | **Courtesy and impatience at priority junctions**: (a) a priority vehicle waiting at its own line that arrived later doesn't block; (b) a slow (< 3 m/s) queued priority driver lets in someone who has waited ≥ 15 s (zipper); (c) at stop/yield lines (not signals) the accepted gap shrinks after 20 s of waiting, to 70 % at 80 s. | Prevents yield-to-right deadlocks and indefinite starvation before the daily warrant review upgrades control. This is documented driver behaviour (waiting-time-dependent gap acceptance). The no-overlap invariant still holds. |
| D10 | 2026-10-05 | **Highways are dual one-way carriageways**; a pass-through (degree-2) node is a normal junction with anticipation of the next stop line. | Simple, uniform model; anticipation removes the short-link dilemma. |
| D11 | 2026-10-06 | **Warrants are reviewed every 5 simulated minutes** (not daily). Volume warrants plus a delay form of the peak-hour warrant: a side-road driver waiting ≥ 90 s while the major street carries ≥ 400 veh/h gives a signal. All-way stops only for balanced, moderate volumes (major < 500 veh/h). | With an 8× clock, a day is 3 sim-hours: a daily review left the first morning rush to jam at junctions that any real city would have signalised. The spec's "e.g. each sim-day" is a suggestion. Five minutes of physical flow is a stable sample. |
| D12 | 2026-10-06 | **Spillback lock**: a vehicle held ≥ 2 min at a line because its exit never has room is reported as a gridlock (detected + logged) and released by taking another exit with room. | In grid spillback, queue tails creep, so there is no wait-for cycle, yet one approach can starve indefinitely. Drivers give up and go another way. |
| D13 | 2026-10-06 | **Police turn round beyond the map edge** when their goal can't be reached inside the map (like D8 for residents). Units park on the shoulder, clear of driveway mouths and junction ends. Civilians ahead of a siren shift up to 1.6 m kerbward and slow to ≤ 1.5 m/s; the unit passes on the centre side from the innermost lane, staying 0.15 m inside the centre line. No offsets within 30 m of a stop line. | The network has no U-turns except at cul-de-sacs. Lateral offsets near junctions conflict with connector geometry. |
| D14 | 2026-10-06 | **Incidents scale with population only** (`incidentsPerThousandPerDay`, default 4), not with the traffic-demand multiplier. Recently patrolled streets are half as likely. | Crime follows residents, not the traffic slider. |
| D15 | 2026-10-06 | **Edits never teleport a vehicle.** After any network change, vehicles on unchanged roads stay. Those on reshaped or split roads are re-anchored by world position. Any vehicle whose pose would still visibly change, or that is parked or pulled over where the kerb moved, **leaves the simulation**, and its occupants are back home. A car already too close to a new junction to stop is committed through it. | The invariants (no teleport, no heading jump, no forced stop) hold during edits too. A car vanishing at the instant the player rebuilds the road under it is far less jarring than one jumping sideways. |
| D16 | 2026-10-06 | **Edit validation.** An edit is rolled back, with a message, if it would create a carriageway under 18 m between junctions (8 m for a roundabout ring segment), a bend tighter than 8 m, or roads meeting at less than 20°. An edit may not shorten an already-short stub. Driveways need a street ≥ 30 m and sit ≥ 12 m from a junction. New roundabouts grow (up to +16 m radius) until every ring segment is ≥ 24 m of arc. | A car's body spanning three track pieces, or a lane offset across a kink, cannot be driven smoothly. These are the geometric limits a real highway authority would impose too. |
| D17 | 2026-10-06 | **Signals keep their cycle across edits.** A coordinated signal's cycle position derives from sim time. An actuated controller resting in all-red re-serves its own group when the other group's calls disappear. Approaches with no phase slot (a fifth leg, a skewed fourth) run *permitted* with the side-street phase. | Fuzzing found edits that starved or froze phases indefinitely. |
| D18 | 2026-10-06 | **Release builds of the engine use `-enforce-exclusivity=unchecked`.** `Edge` and `Connector` are immutable final classes. String-backed enums compare and hash by case tag (`Core/FastEquality.swift`). | Profiling (callgrind) showed 20 % of the step in dynamic exclusivity checks, over 10 % in `RawRepresentable` string comparisons, and heavy retain/release from copying edges. Debug builds and tests keep exclusivity checking. Trace hashes are unchanged. |
| D19 | 2026-10-06 | **Empty Land** sandbox map: seeded river, lake and park, plus one regional road ending in a cul-de-sac. Traffic appears only once the player builds homes and workplaces. | The brief asks for an empty-land option, and it is the natural start for the "build a town from scratch" UI test. |
| D20 | 2026-10-06 | **Saves** are JSON files in Application Support/Cities, written atomically. Autosave runs every in-game hour and on backgrounding. Loading validates first (schema, config, references), and a damaged file is listed and fails with an alert. | Simple, inspectable, robust. Bit-identical resume is tested in `PersistenceTests`. |
| D21 | 2026-10-06 | The **overlay control is a single button that cycles** map → congestion → police coverage, not a menu. | Menu items were placed off-screen in landscape on small iPhones, which was unreliable both for players and for UI tests. |
| D22 | 2026-10-06 | **Building placement snaps** to the nearest valid plot within 14 m of the tap (rings of 4 m). The ghost flashes red with a haptic when nothing fits. | Taps are imprecise on a phone; this matches the brief's "auto-orient their driveway to the nearest road". |
| D23 | 2026-10-06 | **No hairpin movements.** A turn into an adjacent leg whose turn path would be tighter than a 4 m radius is left out of the junction when the approach has any other exit. | Two roads meeting at ~30° made a 150° "right turn" with a 0.1 m radius that no car can drive. Real junctions ban these movements too. |
| D24 | 2026-10-06 | **Pulling over and back.** A car pulled over for a siren eases back as soon as no vehicle overlaps it alongside. A queued follower or a leader just ahead doesn't count, because the shift moves the whole body sideways. A siren car changing lanes passes a yielding car only if it is clear of it at the end of the move as well. | The wider "alongside" margin deadlocked queues: the pulled-over car waited for its follower, and the follower waited for it. |
| D25 | 2026-10-06 | **Starvation counts as a lock.** A car held at a stop line for over 3 minutes, for any mix of reasons (red, conflicts, an exit that is full whenever it turns green), is treated like a spillback lock (D12): it takes another exit, or it is reported as a gridlock together with the queue behind it. A route re-plan keeps a junction commitment the car can no longer comfortably stop for. | Under 3× demand, intermittent starvation never met the strict "exit full" test, and queues sat for over 5 minutes unreported. |
| D26 | 2026-10-06 | **Roads may not overlap.** Validation also rejects an edit that leaves two roads on the same level with overlapping carriageways away from a shared junction (e.g. a dead end stopping inside another road), or a dead end whose turning bulb reaches another road. Interchange ramps are exempt (the invariants cover them). | Fuzzing produced stubs ending inside other roads and bulbs whose U-turn swept across a neighbouring street. |
| D27 | 2026-10-06 | **Fallback routes end at a real map exit.** When a car has no way to its destination (after a missed turn, a gridlock release or an edit), it heads for the cheapest regional connection, or keeps its destination and plans again from the next road. A road in the middle of the network is never treated as "the map edge". Through traffic is only spawned on an entry lane while no car is on, or committed to, a connector into it. | A car told to "leave" at the end of an ordinary road drove off it into the junction beyond, and entry roads that also take junction traffic spawned cars in front of arriving ones. |
| D28 | 2026-10-07 | **Traffic level** is `config.demandMultiplier` (0.25–5). Raising it gives residents who stayed home the remainder of today's plan at once. Through traffic per regional entry is `max(base × level, (level − 1) × 140 veh/h)`. | A player turning the dial expects more cars within minutes, not tomorrow, and a small town must still be able to jam. |
| D29 | 2026-10-07 | **Driveways are driven.** A car leaving follows a path from its building to the mouth on the kerb (`onDriveway`). It waits there at 20° to the traffic for a gap (`drivewayGapOK`), then pulls out in the edge frame with a rear offset solved to keep its heading. An arriving car turns off the lane along a curve into the building. A building close to the road is entered and left from inside (a garage), and the car fades in and out (`Vehicle.visibility`). A stationary queue lets a car out. Cars wait only for a neighbour still moving on its driveway, never for one waiting at its mouth. A car turning in yields only to a car in the stretch of lane it still has to cross. | Cars used to appear and vanish beside the road and slide sideways. Each waiting rule above removed a deadlock found by soak tests at 4× traffic. |
| D30 | 2026-10-07 | **Countryside is scenery,** generated deterministically by the engine (`Simulation.outskirts`) and drawn by the app. It is not simulated: cars leaving the map continue along the country road in the renderer only. Scenery cars head into town at the rate real ones have been arriving on that road. | The town should not end at a hard edge. Simulating the region would cost CPU for nothing the player can influence. |
| D31 | 2026-10-07 | **Road preview by dry run.** `Editor.previewRoad` runs the same build and validation on a scratch copy of the network (about 20 ms on the largest map) and reports what would happen. The app requests one preview at a time while dragging. | One code path for preview and edit, so the preview never disagrees with the result. |
| D32 | 2026-10-08 | **Redraw only what changed.** `RoadNetwork.geometryVersion` moves only when the shape of the network does (carriageways, lanes, junction surfaces, levels, movements); `version` still moves on every rebuild. A junction control switch re-anchors no driveways and redraws only the junction stop and give-way lines. The controller caches road render data, the countryside and each building's driveway strokes. Road edges, markings and arrows are merged into a few shapes by style; road and junction *surfaces* stay one shape each, because SpriteKit fills a compound path even-odd and overlapping surfaces would show holes. Building shadows and sides are untextured sprites (batched). The HUD and the inspector card have their own observable models. | The warrant review switches a control about 26 times an in-game hour on Stress City (one every ~2 s at 10×). Each switch rebuilt every shape on the map, which stalled the main thread for seconds on a simulator (baseline perf run, `1dafb9d`), so taps waited behind it. |
| D33 | 2026-10-08 | **Driveway and merge courtesy.** A car on a driveway looks 8 m along its path and stops short of any vehicle there; when two are each on the other's path the lower id goes first, and a car waiting at its mouth goes first when the car turning in is waiting for it. A car does not leave its garage while anyone is on the first stretch of its driveway or where its body starts. A turning-in car is listed in its lane where its front is. Cars on merging turn paths follow whoever is further along the shared zone. A siren car keeps its offset while stepping back would touch anyone. | Soaking Stress City (2,000+ cars, both driving sides) found each of these as an overlap; long back-row driveways run past other houses' mouths and garages, which no shorter rule covered. |
